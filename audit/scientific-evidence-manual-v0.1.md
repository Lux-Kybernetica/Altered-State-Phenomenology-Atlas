## Core scientific anchors

### Bodily self-consciousness

- Blanke et al. 2002 — focal stimulation and illusory own-body perceptions;
- Ehrsson 2007 — experimental induction of an OBE-like self-location illusion;
- Lenggenhager et al. 2007 — multisensory manipulation of body ownership and self-location;
- Ionta et al. 2011 — self-location / first-person perspective and temporo-parietal mechanisms.

### Sleep onset

- Hayashi, Katoh & Hori 1999 — hypnagogic imagery varies with EEG-defined sleep-onset stages.

### Sleep paralysis / vestibular-motor phenomenology

- Sharpless & Barber 2011 — systematic review of lifetime sleep-paralysis prevalence;
- Cheyne & Girard 2009 — vestibular-motor hallucinations and OBE-like phenomenology during sleep paralysis.

### Lucid dreaming and measurement

- Voss et al. 2009 — signal-verified lucid REM EEG study;
- Baird, Tononi & LaBerge 2022 — artifact-corrected reanalysis weakening the original 40-Hz/hybrid-state interpretation;
- Dresler et al. 2012 — combined EEG/fMRI lucid-REM case study;
- Konkoly et al. 2021 — two-way communication during polysomnographically verified REM dreams.

## Deliberate scientific disagreement retained

The evidence layer keeps both Voss et al. 2009 and Baird et al. 2022.

This is intentional. The later analysis argues that the frontolateral 40-Hz effect used to support a waking/REM `hybrid state` was attributable to eye-movement-related saccadic spike potentials. The database therefore records scientific correction rather than silently presenting the earlier interpretation as settled.

## Boundary rule

Scientific evidence can:

- reproduce a component experimentally;
- provide a plausible mechanism;
- provide clinical or epidemiological context;
- supply a measurement technique;
- challenge a source-framework explanation.

Scientific evidence does **not** become `same_as` a Nova phenomenon by database convention and does not, merely by being neuroscientific, settle the metaphysical interpretation of an experience.
