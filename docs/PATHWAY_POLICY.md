# Experiential Pathway policy

A **Pathway** is not an ontology edge set.

It represents a recurrent, proposed, or observed **temporal route through concepts**. Pathways are allowed to branch, skip stages, contain optional markers, and remain uncertain.

## Why separate pathways from the NC semantic graph?

The semantic graph answers questions such as:

- What kind of concept is this?
- Which concepts are explicitly distinguished?
- Which phenomena are associated in Nova's nomenclature?
- Which action is described as facilitating or inhibiting another concept?

A pathway answers a different question:

> In what sequence can these concepts appear during an experience or protocol?

Mixing the two would turn a recurrent experiential sequence into a fake taxonomic law.

## Rules

1. **No universal-sequence assumption.** A stage can be optional.
2. **No causality from order.** `A before B` does not mean `A causes B`.
3. **Branches are first-class.** Different transition markers can occupy the same functional stage.
4. **Provenance travels with every transition.** Nova wording, Lux observation, Lux hypothesis, and future external evidence remain distinct.
5. **NC nodes are referenced, not rewritten.** A pathway points to canonical `nova.*` term IDs.
6. **Missing NC terms may later become pathway-local concepts**, but only after explicit modeling; they are never silently injected into the NC source layer.
7. **Pathway evidence is cumulative.** Future structured experience reports should allow frequency counts for stage occurrence and transition order.

## Intended quantitative extension

Later each transition may carry observed counts such as:

- number of reports containing both stages;
- number where A preceded B;
- number where B occurred without A;
- induction context;
- confidence / reporting quality.

This will allow recurrent sequences to become empirical models instead of anecdotes arranged into an attractive arrow diagram.
