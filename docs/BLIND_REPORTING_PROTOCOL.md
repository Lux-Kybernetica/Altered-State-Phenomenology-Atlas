# Blind phenomenology capture protocol v0.1

The purpose of this protocol is to reduce **semantic contamination** when testing whether different traditions converge on similar phenomenology.

If a participant is shown terms such as “vibratory state”, “takeoff”, “psychosome”, “Focus 10” or “sleep paralysis” before describing the experience, later vocabulary agreement cannot be treated as independent convergence.

## Capture order

### 1. Freeze the raw report first

Collect an uninterrupted free description before showing Nova terminology or any Monroe/Vieira vocabulary.

Preferred prompt:

> Describe what happened in the order you remember it. Use your own words, including bodily sensations, sounds, images, changes in location or perspective, actions you attempted, and how the episode ended.

Do not name expected phenomena in this phase.

### 2. Neutral clarification only

If clarification is needed, ask questions that do not supply the answer category.

Preferred forms:

- “What changed next?”
- “Where did you feel that?”
- “Did it feel continuous or intermittent?”
- “Could you move physically at that point?”
- “What made you think the scene or position had changed?”

Avoid forms such as:

- “Did you feel vibrations?”
- “Was that a partial exit?”
- “Did you enter Focus 10?”
- “Was your psychosome separating?”

The latter prompts seed the vocabulary we later claim to discover. A venerable human research technique, unfortunately.

### 3. Record prior framework exposure separately

After the raw narrative is frozen, record prior exposure to relevant frameworks or communities, for example:

- Gateway / Monroe;
- Projectiology / Vieira;
- lucid dreaming;
- OBE communities;
- meditation/yoga traditions;
- sleep-paralysis literature;
- other relevant systems.

Record level as `none`, `minimal`, `familiar`, `trained`, or `practitioner`.

Exposure does not invalidate a report. It changes how strongly vocabulary convergence can be interpreted as independent.

### 4. Record elicitation conditions

Classify the report as:

- `spontaneous_free_report`
- `open_interview`
- `structured_neutral_prompts`
- `term_prompted`

Also record whether Nova or external-tradition terms were shown before the raw report.

### 5. Map to Nova only after freezing raw text

The analyst may then assign canonical NC term references.

The original description is never rewritten to make the mapping cleaner.

If a phrase does not fit a Nova term, retain the phrase and allow an empty or uncertain mapping. Ontologies exist to serve observations, not the reverse, despite centuries of bureaucratic evidence.

### 6. Keep interpretation separate

Phenomenological coding and interpretation are different fields.

Example:

Raw description:

> “My whole body seemed to buzz in waves, but I don't think I was physically shaking.”

Possible NC mapping:

- `nova.etat_vibratoire`

Possible interpretations, stored separately:

- sleep-transition sensory phenomenon;
- energetic interpretation;
- Projectiology interpretation;
- Gateway interpretation;
- unknown.

The mapping does not adjudicate the interpretation.

### 7. Record capture delay

Whenever possible, record how long after the experience the narrative was captured.

A report written immediately and a story reconstructed years later are both potentially useful, but they must not silently receive the same memory weight.

### 8. Prefer independent double coding for high-value reports

For reports used in convergence statistics, two independent analysts should ideally map the frozen raw report before reconciliation.

Disagreements are data. Preserve them before consensus.

## Independence strata

For later analysis, reports can be stratified rather than simply accepted/rejected.

### Lower semantic-contamination risk

- free or neutral report;
- no NC/external terms shown beforehand;
- little or no prior exposure to the mapped tradition;
- raw report frozen before coding.

### Higher semantic-contamination risk

- term-prompted interview;
- extensive prior training in the vocabulary being matched;
- external/Nova labels shown before free description;
- analyst rewrites replacing participant language.

High-exposure reports are still useful for studying trained phenomenology and techniques. They simply cannot carry the same weight when asking whether a vocabulary convergence arose independently.

## Minimum rule

**Raw phenomenology first. Vocabulary second. Theory last.**

This ordering is now the default methodological preference for NC × Lux empirical reports.
